Closing the Rural Digital Divide in Scotland

We work to ensure that no rural household is left behind in a digital world.

Reliable internet access, digital skills, and essential online services should not depend on where you live

Why We Exist
Across rural Scotland, many communities face:
    Unstable or no internet connection
    Poor access to NHS and public services online
    Barriers to education and employment opportunities
    Social isolation caused by digital exclusion

Without digital access, everyday life becomes harder and inequality grows.

What We Do
We tackle digital exclusion through:
    Connectivity Support

    Helping households and communities access reliable internet infrastructure.

    Digital Skills Support

    Training people to confidently use online services, devices, and tools.

    Devices for Inclusion

    Provision of refurbished or donated devices to those in need.

    Community Partnerships

    Working with community organisations, schools, councils, Healthcare providers, and charities.

    Research & Mapping

    Identifying where digital inequality is most severe in Scotland.

About Us

Skywave RuralNet SCIO is a Scottish Charitable Incorporated Organisation (SCIO) dedicated to reducing digital exclusion across rural Scotland. We believe that reliable digital connectivity is essential for modern life. Yet many rural communities continue to face barriers to accessing the internet, digital services, education, healthcare, employment, and opportunities for economic growth. Our work brings together research, community engagement, and practical action to develop sustainable solutions that improve digital inclusion. We combine evidence-based connectivity initiatives, community access hubs, and digital skills support to help ensure rural communities are connected, confident, and able to benefit from the digital world. Our approach is informed by original research, including postcode-level connectivity mapping and academic work undertaken in collaboration with The Mhor Collective. This evidence helps us understand local challenges and design solutions that reflect the needs of rural communities. We are currently developing a pilot programme in Sutherland and Wester Ross, working alongside local communities, partners, and stakeholders to create practical, scalable models that can be adapted across rural Scotland.

Our Mission

To reduce digital exclusion in rural Scotland by developing evidence-based, community-led solutions that improve connectivity, increase digital confidence, and create equal opportunities for everyone to participate in the digital world.

Our Vision

A rural Scotland where every community has access to reliable digital connectivity, the skills and confidence to use it effectively, and equal opportunities to thrive socially, economically, and culturally in an increasingly digital society.

Research & Evidence

Our work is based on a postcode-level spatial analysis of broadband inequality across rural Scotland using premises-level data.

2026 Pilot Programme

Our pilot programme focuses on postcode districts IV26, IV27, and IV28 in rural Scotland.

Key Finding

Broadband inequality is spatially clustered and cannot be understood using simple "served vs underserved" classification.

IV26

Large number of premises experience intermediate broadband connectivity requiring targetted improvement

IV27

Significant Variation exists between well served and under served communities

IV28

The highest level of broadband deprivation identified in our study
